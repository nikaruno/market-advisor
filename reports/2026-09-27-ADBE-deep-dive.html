The business

Adobe sells subscription software for creativity (Creative Cloud: Photoshop, Premiere, Illustrator, Firefly generative AI), document productivity (Acrobat and Document Cloud) and customer-experience management for enterprises (Experience Platform, analytics, commerce). Almost all revenue is subscription-based. From fiscal 2026, Adobe reports a single operating segment and discloses revenue by customer group: Creative & Marketing Professionals, and Business Professionals & Consumers. The segment figures below are therefore the last three-segment split, for FY2025.

  • Q3 FY2026 (quarter ended 2026-08-28): revenue $6.76B (+13% y/y), GAAP EPS $4.62, non-GAAP EPS $6.13, total ARR $27.50B, RPO $22.16B (67% current). Source: Q3 FY26 press release (8-K), 2026-09-10.
  • Customer-group subscription revenue in Q3 FY26: Creative & Marketing Professionals $4.65B (+13%); Business Professionals & Consumers $1.91B (+16%). Source: same press release.
  • Updated FY2026 targets: revenue $26.576–26.626B, GAAP EPS $18.12–18.17, non-GAAP EPS $24.45–24.50, ARR growth 10.2%. Q4 targets: revenue $6.80–6.85B, GAAP EPS $4.65–4.70. Source: 8-K, 2026-09-10.
  • Adobe says AI-first ARR exceeded $650M, up more than 150% y/y. This is a company-defined metric and is small next to total ARR of $27.5B.
  • Segment reporting changed to a single segment from Q1 FY2026 (FY2025 10-K). This makes it harder to track the enterprise Digital Experience business separately from the creative business.
  • Capital return: $24.55B remained under the April 2026 repurchase authorization (runs through 2030-04-30). About 9.5M shares were repurchased in Q3. No dividend. Source: Q3 FY26 10-Q.

Risks and catalysts

Risks

  • Generative-AI disruption of the Creative Cloud seat model — Adobe's 10-K risk factors cite competition, including from AI-native products, and the difficulty of implementing and monetising generative AI. If AI lowers the skill needed for creative work, fewer professional seats and lower price points could follow. The market appears to price this already (P/E 12.9x TTM, 1-year return -34.7%). Reported results do not yet show it in revenue (+12.9% y/y), but they may show it in margins. (Adobe Inc. Form 10-K FY2025, Risk Factors (SEC))
  • Leadership transition and executive turnover — Anil Chakravarthy, who runs the Customer Experience Orchestration business and worldwide field operations, becomes CEO on 2026-12-01. Shantanu Narayen moves to Executive Chair after 18 years as CEO. David Wadhwani, head of the creativity and productivity business, announced his departure around 2026-09-04; that report comes from an aggregator citing his LinkedIn post. The change happens at a moment of strategic pressure on the creative franchise. (Adobe newsroom press release (2026-09-03); Yahoo Finance (aggregator, 2026-09-04) for the Wadhwani departure)
  • Subscription-practice regulation (DOJ/ROSCA injunction) — On 2026-03-13 Adobe settled the DOJ lawsuit, referred by the FTC, over early-termination fees and cancellation friction: $75M civil penalty plus $75M in free services. The injunction requires clear disclosure of early-termination fees, reminders before free trials convert, and easy cancellation. Churn could rise at the margin. Adobe denies wrongdoing. (U.S. Department of Justice, Office of Public Affairs press release)
  • Profit growth lagging revenue growth — In the quarter to 2026-08-31, revenue grew 12.9% y/y, while operating income grew 8.3% and net income only 3.1%. Operating margin was 34.8% vs 36.3%. If AI compute and go-to-market costs keep rising, EPS growth will depend increasingly on buybacks. Figures are from the brief's quarterly statements; headline revenue matches the Q3 press release. (market-advisor valuation pipeline (quarterly statements to 2026-08-31); Adobe Q3 FY26 press release (8-K, 2026-09-10))
  • Buybacks funded beyond free cash flow; balance sheet has moved to net debt — FY25 buybacks of $11.28B exceeded FCF of $9.85B. Total debt rose from $4.08B (FY23) to $6.76B (2026-08-31), and the latest quarter shows net debt of $1.12B against net cash earlier. $24.55B of repurchase authority remains. The Q3 10-Q shows $1.60B of debt classified as current, including notes due 2027. Leverage is still low, about 0.11x EBITDA. The risk is that per-share growth is being bought rather than earned. (Adobe Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q (SEC); market-advisor quality/valuation pipelines)
  • Reduced disclosure after segment consolidation — From Q1 FY2026, Adobe reports a single segment instead of Digital Media / Digital Experience / Publishing & Advertising. Investors can no longer see directly whether the enterprise Digital Experience business (FY25 $5.86B, +9%) is slowing. Weakness in one area could therefore be masked by the other. (Adobe Inc. Form 10-K FY2025 (SEC))

Reading it together

What the data says

  • Quality is top-tier: score 75.5, 95.3rd percentile of 129 scored companies, 100% data completeness over 4 years. Average ROIC is 45.4% and FCF margin 39.2%, the highest FCF margin among all peer candidates in the brief.
  • Growth is steady but not fast: 10.5% revenue CAGR over FY22–FY25 and +12.9% y/y in the latest quarter. In that quarter operating income grew only 8.3% and net income 3.1%.
  • Valuation is the lowest among the chosen peers: EV/EBITDA 9.3x (normalized 9.35x), P/E 12.85x TTM and 8.51x forward, vs INTU 10.9x / 16.1x and CRM 13.6x / 19.9x.
  • The price trend is negative: close $235.47 (2026-09-25), below the 50-day ($257.42) and 200-day ($262.68) averages, down 34.7% over one year and 13.9% over one month. Technical rating is Strong Sell.
  • Capital return is aggressive: FY25 buybacks of $11.28B exceeded FCF of $9.85B. Diluted shares fell 9.3% over three years. Latest-quarter net debt is $1.12B.

What it might mean

  • The market is valuing Adobe less as a compounder than as a franchise at risk of AI disruption. A P/E around 13x TTM implies low or no long-run growth for a business still growing revenue by double digits.
  • The gap between revenue growth (12.9%) and net-income growth (3.1%) may be the first sign of AI costs or competitive spending compressing margins. If it persists, the low multiple partly reflects reality.
  • At these prices, buybacks retire shares at roughly an 8–12% earnings yield. That supports per-share growth if the core business holds, but it amplifies losses if it does not.
  • Leadership change is a double-edged catalyst. The new CEO comes from the enterprise and customer-experience side, not the creative side, and the creative business lead has departed. That could signal a strategic reset or add execution risk right when the creative franchise is under the most pressure.
  • Among these peers, Adobe offers the most cash per dollar of market value. NOW and CDNS offer faster growth, and the market is paying a large premium for that growth and perceived AI-beneficiary status.

What is uncertain / counter-case

  • Value-trap risk is live. Low multiples, falling price and slowing profit growth are exactly what a durable decline in the creative seat base would look like early on. Revenue has not yet shown it, but subscription businesses tend to show damage in revenue with a lag.
  • The profitability metrics are flattered. ROIC of 45% and P/B of 7.96 are inflated by equity shrunk through buybacks, and FY24 earnings are depressed by the $1B Figma fee, which overstates the FY24→FY25 EPS jump (+35%).
  • Net-debt figures conflict. The quality pipeline shows net cash (net debt/EBITDA -0.06x), while latest-quarter data show net debt of $1.12B. The brief's total debt ($6.76B) also differs from the 10-Q's total notes figure ($6.36B), possibly because of leases or other items. Leverage is low on any measure.
  • Peer comparisons are imperfect. Adobe's closest creative competitors (Canva, Figma, Microsoft, Google) are not in the universe, so 'cheapest among peers' compares Adobe with different businesses. Quality scores are pool-relative percentiles over only 129 companies.
  • Company-defined AI metrics (AI-first ARR above $650M) are unaudited and small relative to $27.5B total ARR. They show adoption, not whether AI adds to or cannibalises the core subscription base.
  • The counter-case to the bear view: revenue growth accelerated from about 10% in FY25 to 12.9% in the latest quarter, guidance was raised, and RPO is $22.16B. The sell-off may be sentiment-driven. The technical pipeline cannot distinguish the two.
